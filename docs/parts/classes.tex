\chapter{Package \& Class Usage}\label{C:Classes}

	\rpgtex{} may be loaded through one of its four classes, which set up the whole document, or as a package within a document of any other class. Most documents should use a class. The simplest complete \rpgtex{} document is:
	\begin{Code}
		\cmd*{documentclass}\{rpgbook\}

		\cmd*{begin}\{document\}

		~~\cmd*{chapter}\{The Adventure Begins\}

		~~Your text here.

		\cmd*{end}\{document\}
	\end{Code}
	Options are given in the usual way, such as \cmd*{documentclass}[theme=dnd, columns=1]\{rpgbook\} \RpgPage[p]{S:PackageOptions}.

	\section{Choosing a Class}\label{S:ChooseClass}

		Each class is suited to a different kind of document:
		\begin{RpgTable}{lX}
			Class & Purpose
			\\
			\texttt{rpgbook} \RpgPage[p]{S:bookClass} & Long documents, such as rulebooks and adventures, which are divided into parts and chapters.
			\\
			\texttt{rpgcard} \RpgPage[p]{S:cardClass} & One or a few cards, such as an item or spell, cropped to size for viewing on screen.
			\\
			\texttt{rpgdeck} \RpgPage[p]{S:deckClass} & Many cards, arranged on pages for printing.
			\\
			\texttt{rpghandout} \RpgPage[p]{S:handoutClass} & Shorter documents, such as player handouts, which are divided into sections.
		\end{RpgTable}

		\subsection{What the Classes Provide}\label{S:ClassCommon}

			Compared with loading the package, every class:
			\begin{itemize}
				\item Activates the \texttt{font}, \texttt{background} and \texttt{sections} modules \RpgPage[p]{S:PackageOptions}. Every class except \texttt{rpgcard} also activates the \texttt{title} module.
				\item Sets the page size (A4, except in the screen mode of \texttt{rpgcard}) and margins, and passes the \texttt{size}, \texttt{columns}, \texttt{openright} and \texttt{oneside} options to the underlying class.
				\item Applies \rpgtex{}'s overall text layout: hyphenation is restricted, text is left-aligned unless the \texttt{justified} option is given, lists are spaced more compactly, and the table of contents is set in two columns unless the \texttt{nomultitoc} option is given.
			\end{itemize}

		\subsection{Font Sizes}\label{S:ClassSizes}

			The \texttt{size} option sets the base font size, which must be one of the sizes accepted by the underlying class:
			\begin{RpgTable}{lX}
				Classes & Sizes
				\\
				\texttt{rpgbook}, \texttt{rpghandout} & \texttt{8pt}, \texttt{9pt}, \texttt{10pt}, \texttt{11pt}, \texttt{12pt}, \texttt{14pt}, \texttt{17pt} and \texttt{20pt}
				\\
				\texttt{rpgcard}, \texttt{rpgdeck} & \texttt{10pt}, \texttt{11pt} and \texttt{12pt}
			\end{RpgTable}

	\section{Standalone Package}\label{S:StandalonePackage}

		The package may be loaded into a document of any class:
		\begin{Code}
			\cmd*{documentclass}\{<any-class>\}

			\cmd*{usepackage}[<options>]\{rpgtex\}
		\end{Code}
		This loads the core commands and environments, and the theme (the \texttt{default} theme, unless the \texttt{theme} option is used). However, the modules which change the fonts, page backgrounds, section headings and title page are inactive unless they are enabled with the \texttt{font}, \texttt{background}, \texttt{sections} and \texttt{title} options, and none of the layout changes listed above \RpgPage[p]{S:ClassCommon} are made. This allows \rpgtex{} to be added to an existing document without changing its overall appearance.

	\section{rpgbook}\label{S:bookClass}

		The \texttt{rpgbook} class is designed for long documents, such as rulebooks, sourcebooks and adventures, which need to be organised into parts and chapters. It is the class used to typeset this manual.

		It is based on the \texttt{extbook} class from the \forcelink{https://ctan.org/pkg/extsizes}{extsizes} package, which behaves almost identically to the standard \texttt{book} class, but accepts a wider range of font sizes \RpgPage[p]{S:ClassSizes}. All of the usual \texttt{book} commands are therefore available, including \cmd*{part}, \cmd*{chapter}, \cmd*{frontmatter}, \cmd*{mainmatter}, \cmd*{backmatter} and \cmd*{appendix}.

		By default, \cmd{maketitle} produces a full cover page \RpgPage[p]{Cmd:RpgThemeTitleMode}. The \texttt{openright} option starts each chapter on a right-hand page, and the \texttt{oneside} option lays the document out for single-sided printing.

		The page geometry is:
		\begin{RpgTable}{ll}
			Element & Size
			\\
			Left and right margins & 0.65in
			\\
			Top margin & 0.4in
			\\
			Bottom margin (from the text to the bottom of the page) & 0.75in
			\\
			Footer separation (from the text to the footer) & 0.3in
			\\
			Gap between columns & 0.25in
		\end{RpgTable}

	\section{rpghandout}\label{S:handoutClass}

		The \texttt{rpghandout} class is designed for shorter documents, where the structure of a book is unnecessary: printouts for players, or short adventure modules.

		It is based on the \texttt{extarticle} class from the \forcelink{https://ctan.org/pkg/extsizes}{extsizes} package, which behaves almost identically to the standard \texttt{article} class, but accepts a wider range of font sizes \RpgPage[p]{S:ClassSizes}. The highest sectioning level is therefore \cmd*{section}.

		By default, \cmd{maketitle} produces a compact title at the top of the first page, rather than a cover page \RpgPage[p]{Cmd:RpgThemeTitleMode}. It is often followed by a short summary, using the \texttt{abstract} environment \RpgPage[p]{S:Abstracts}.

		The page geometry is the same as that of \texttt{rpgbook} \RpgPage[p]{S:bookClass}.

	\section{rpgcard}\label{S:cardClass}

		The \texttt{rpgcard} class is designed for typesetting one or a few cards \RpgPage[p]{Env:RpgCard}, primarily for viewing on screen, or for collecting into a deck later \RpgPage[p]{S:deckClass}. The rule environments are displayed as cards: the class turns on the switch of every cardable rule environment \RpgPage[p]{C:ThemeRuleEnvs} which exists when the class is loaded. This includes the standard environments, such as \texttt{RpgItem} and \texttt{RpgSpell}, and any created by the theme given in the class options, but not those created later (in the preamble, or by a theme loaded within the document), whose switches must be turned on with \cmdref{RpgSwitch}. Other switches, such as \texttt{ShowSecrets}, are unaffected.

		The class has two modes, chosen by the \texttt{bg} option \RpgPage[p]{S:PackageOptions}:
		\begin{description}
			\item[Screen mode] (\texttt{bg=full}, the default): the class is based on the \texttt{standalone} class, and each page is cropped to fit its contents exactly, giving a file which contains just the card.
			\item[Print mode] (\texttt{bg=print} or \texttt{bg=none}): the class is based on the \texttt{article} class, and the cards are placed on A4 pages, with a margin of 5pt on each side. For printing large numbers of cards, \texttt{rpgdeck} is more suitable.
		\end{description}

		Cards cannot have a title page or a table of contents, and \cmd{maketitle} raises an error. The \texttt{columns} option is ignored, since cards are always laid out in a single column.

		In screen mode, the whole document is typeset as a single line, so everything should be placed within a card or rule environment: loose text and headings outside of a card cause errors. Several cards are placed side by side.

	\section{rpgdeck}\label{S:deckClass}

		The \texttt{rpgdeck} class is designed to collect a large number of cards \RpgPage[p]{Env:RpgCard} into a single document for printing, including cards which were written as separate \texttt{rpgcard} documents.

		It is based on the standard \texttt{article} class, on A4 pages with a margin of 5pt on each side, and the cards are placed side by side, filling each page in turn. As in \texttt{rpgcard}, the rule environments are displayed as cards: the class turns on the switch of every cardable rule environment which exists when the class is loaded, including the standard ones and any created by the theme given in the class options. Environments created later (in the preamble, or by a theme loaded within the document) must have their switches turned on with \cmdref{RpgSwitch}. The \texttt{columns} option is ignored, and no page numbers or footers are shown. Unlike \texttt{rpgcard}, a deck may have a title: \cmd{maketitle} produces a compact title at the top of the first page \RpgPage[p]{Cmd:RpgThemeTitleMode}.

		With the default \texttt{bg=full}, the theme's paper background is drawn behind the cards across the whole page. When printing, \texttt{bg=print} avoids this.

		\subsection{Including rpgcard Documents}

			The \texttt{rpgdeck} class loads the \forcelink{https://ctan.org/pkg/standalone}{\texttt{standalone} package} with the \texttt{subpreambles=true} option. This allows a complete \texttt{rpgcard} document, with its own \cmd*{documentclass} and \texttt{document} environment, to be included in a deck with \cmd*{input}. For example, given the card:
			\begin{Code}
				\% spell1.tex

				\cmd*{documentclass}[theme=dnd]\{rpgcard\}

				\cmd*{begin}\{document\}

				~~\cmd*{begin}\{RpgSpell\}\{Fireball\}

				~~~~\ldots

				~~\cmd*{end}\{RpgSpell\}

				\cmd*{end}\{document\}
			\end{Code}
			which may be compiled on its own (and, for example, sent to a player), several such cards may be collected into a deck:
			\begin{Code}
				\% deck.tex

				\cmd*{documentclass}[theme=dnd, bg=print]\{rpgdeck\}

				\cmd*{begin}\{document\}

				~~\cmd*{input}\{spell1\}

				~~\cmd*{input}\{spell2\}

				~~\ldots

				\cmd*{end}\{document\}
			\end{Code}

			The preambles of the included cards are collected and added to the preamble of the deck, so any definitions they make are shared between all of the cards. Two cards which define the same command therefore conflict, and every card uses the last definition. To avoid this, definitions which differ between cards should be made within their \texttt{document} environments.

			The \cmd*{documentclass} line of an included card is ignored, along with its options. The cards are therefore typeset with the deck's theme and options, which may differ from the ones used when the card was compiled on its own; \cmdref{RpgSetTheme} may be used within the deck to change the theme.
